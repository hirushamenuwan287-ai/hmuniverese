What Is the Universe?

The universe includes everything we know to exist: space, time, matter, energy, stars, planets, galaxies, black holes and countless other objects.

Modern observations indicate that the universe has been expanding for billions of years. Scientists study this expansion to understand how the cosmos evolved.

The observable universe is only the region from which light has had enough time to reach us. What exists beyond that observable region remains an important subject of cosmological research.

Cosmic Timeline

A simplified journey from the early universe to today.

13.8 Billion Years Ago

The early universe was extremely hot and dense and began expanding.

First Atoms

As the universe cooled, particles combined to form simple atoms.

First Stars

Gravity gathered clouds of gas into dense regions where the first stars eventually formed.

Galaxies

Stars became organized into huge galaxies containing billions of stars.

4.6 Billion Years Ago

Our Sun and Solar System formed from a rotating cloud of gas and dust.

Present Day

Humans use telescopes and spacecraft to study the universe and search for other worlds.

Our Solar System

Eight planets orbit our Sun, along with moons, asteroids, comets and other objects.

Mercury

The smallest planet and the closest planet to the Sun.

Venus

A rocky planet with an extremely thick and hot atmosphere.

Earth

Our home world and the only world currently known to support life.

Mars

The red planet with enormous volcanoes and valleys.

Jupiter

The largest planet in our Solar System.

Saturn

A gas giant famous for its spectacular rings.

Uranus

An ice giant with an unusual extreme axial tilt.

Neptune

A distant blue ice giant with powerful winds.

The Sun

The star at the center of our Solar System.

Energy

The Sun produces energy through nuclear fusion in its core.

Gravity

The Sun's gravity keeps the planets and many other objects in orbit around it.

Solar Activity

The Sun produces solar flares and other activity that can affect space weather near Earth.

Earth's Moon

Our planet's natural satellite and a major target of human space exploration.

Phases

The Moon appears to change shape throughout its cycle because we see different portions illuminated by sunlight.

Tides

The Moon's gravity plays an important role in Earth's ocean tides.

Exploration

Robotic spacecraft and human missions have helped scientists study the lunar surface.

Stars

Stars are enormous balls of hot plasma powered primarily by nuclear fusion.

Star Formation

Stars form when gravity causes clouds of gas and dust to collapse into dense regions.

Stellar Evolution

A star's mass strongly influences how it evolves and how it eventually ends its life.

Supernova

Some massive stars end their lives in enormous explosions that can distribute elements into space.

Galaxies

Vast systems containing stars, gas, dust and dark matter.

Spiral Galaxies

Spiral galaxies have rotating disks and curved arms. The Milky Way is a spiral galaxy.

Elliptical Galaxies

These galaxies have smoother shapes and often contain many older stars.

Milky Way

Our Solar System is located inside the Milky Way, a galaxy containing a huge number of stars.

Black Holes

Some of the most extreme objects known in the universe.

Event Horizon

The event horizon is the boundary beyond which escaping from the black hole is impossible.

Stellar Black Holes

These can form when massive stars collapse at the end of their lives.

Supermassive Black Holes

Huge black holes with millions or billions of solar masses can exist at galaxy centers.

Other Cosmic Objects

The universe contains much more than stars and planets.

Asteroids

Rocky or metallic objects that orbit the Sun. Many are found in the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter.

Comets

Icy objects that can develop bright comas and tails when they approach the Sun.

Nebulae

Huge clouds of gas and dust where stars can form or where material from dying stars can spread.

Exoplanets

Worlds orbiting stars beyond our Solar System.

What Are Exoplanets?

Exoplanets are planets that orbit stars other than our Sun. Thousands have been confirmed.

How We Find Them

Astronomers can detect exoplanets by observing changes in starlight or the movement of their host stars.

Searching for Life

Scientists study some exoplanets to determine whether their environments might have conditions suitable for life.

The Invisible Universe

Much of the universe does not directly interact with light in ways that allow us to see it.

Dark Matter

Dark matter is a term for unseen matter inferred from gravitational effects on visible objects.

Dark Energy

Dark energy is the name given to the unknown phenomenon associated with the accelerated expansion of the universe.

Why It Matters

Understanding these components is one of the major challenges in modern cosmology.

Great Cosmic Mysteries

Questions that scientists are still working to answer.

Is There Life Elsewhere?

Earth is currently the only known world with life. Scientists continue searching for signs of life beyond our planet.

What Is Dark Matter?

We can observe its gravitational effects, but its fundamental nature remains unknown.

What Is Dark Energy?

The cause of the universe's accelerated expansion is still not fully understood.

What Happened at the Beginning?

Scientists have models describing the early universe, but the earliest moments remain an area of active research.

What Happens Inside Black Holes?

Extreme gravity creates conditions that challenge our current understanding of physics.

Are We Alone?

The search for life and intelligent civilizations remains one of humanity's biggest scientific questions.

Humanity in Space

From the first satellites to robotic explorers and space telescopes, humans continue to expand our reach.

Space Telescopes

Space telescopes observe the universe from above Earth's atmosphere and can study many different wavelengths of light.

Robotic Missions

Robotic spacecraft have visited planets, moons, asteroids and distant regions of our Solar System.

Human Spaceflight

Humans have lived and worked in orbit and have explored the Moon through crewed missions.

Space Stations

Orbiting laboratories allow scientists to perform experiments in microgravity.

Mars Exploration

Robotic explorers have studied the Martian surface, atmosphere, rocks and ancient environments.

Future Exploration

Future missions may explore the Moon, Mars, asteroids and other destinations.
